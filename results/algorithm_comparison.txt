ALGORITHM AND PROTOCOL COMPARISON

1. TCP VS UDP

Observation:
TCP provided reliable delivery using acknowledgements and
retransmission mechanisms. UDP transmitted data without
connection-oriented reliability mechanisms. The traffic
generation methods differed between these experiments.

3. GO-BACK-N ARQ

Initial Transmission Attempts: 8
Successfully Transmitted Initially: 7
Packets Deliberately Lost: 1
Packets Retransmitted: 4
Out-of-Order Packets Discarded: 3
Duplicate ACKs: 3
Timeouts: 1
Total Packets Delivered: 8

Observation:
When a packet was lost, the receiver discarded subsequent
out-of-order packets. After a timeout, the sender retransmitted
all outstanding packets. All 8 packets were eventually delivered.

4. LEAKY BUCKET ALGORITHM

Total Incoming Packets: 13
Packets Accepted: 10
Packets Dropped: 3
Packets Transmitted: 10
Packets Remaining: 0
Bucket Capacity: 4 Packets
Leakage Interval: 0.5 Seconds

Observation:
The Leaky Bucket algorithm controlled outgoing traffic
at a constant rate. Packets arriving when the bucket was
full were dropped. All accepted packets were transmitted.

OVERALL CONCLUSION

The experiments demonstrate different networking concepts:
- TCP and UDP demonstrate transport-layer communication.
- TCP Sliding Window demonstrates flow control.
- Go-Back-N ARQ demonstrates reliable data transmission.
- Leaky Bucket demonstrates traffic shaping.

These experiments use different simulation models and
metrics. Their numerical results should not be treated
as a direct performance ranking.
